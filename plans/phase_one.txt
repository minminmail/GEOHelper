If SEO is done, your site is already crawlable, indexed and ranking. 
GEO work is mostly about three new things: being quotable, being mentioned elsewhere, 
and measuring what AI actually says about you. Here's what to add, in priority order.

Phase 1: Baseline audit (week 1)
Build a prompt list. Write 20–50 questions your buyers would ask an AI,
 e.g. "best GenAI consultants in Spain", 
 "how to deploy a multi-agent system on Azure", 
 "who can help with Kafka to KRaft migration".
Test them across engines. 
ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews. 
Log whether you're mentioned, which competitors appear, which sources get cited, and whether facts about you are correct.
Identify the cited sources. 
The domains AI keeps citing for your topics are your real GEO targets. 
Often they're not the sites that rank #1 on Google.

Phase 2: Make existing content quotable (weeks 2–4)
Add answer-first summaries.
 Put a 2–3 sentence direct answer at the top of each key page, before the detail.
Rewrite headings as questions that match your prompt list.
Add quotable facts: concrete numbers, results, timelines, named methods. 
"Reduced response time from 8s to 1.2s" gets cited; "improves performance" doesn't.
Add FAQ and comparison blocks to service pages.
Strengthen entity schema: Organization, Person (founder with credentials), Service, FAQPage, with sameAs links to LinkedIn, GitHub, Crunchbase, etc.
Check robots.txt allows
 GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot,
  Google-Extended; optionally add an llms.txt summarizing who you are and your key pages.

  Phase 3: Build off-site presence (ongoing, the biggest lever)
Unify your brand description. One canonical paragraph (who, what, where, for whom) used identically on the website, LinkedIn, directories, bios and profiles.
Get listed where AI looks: Clutch, G2, GoodFirms, industry directories, Crunchbase, Wikidata if eligible.
Earn mentions in cited sources found in Phase 1: guest articles, interviews, podcasts, being quoted by journalists.
Participate in communities LLMs weigh heavily: Reddit, Stack Overflow, GitHub, LinkedIn articles, Medium. Genuine expert answers, not promotion.
Publish original material: benchmarks, surveys, open-source tools, case studies. Original data is the single most-cited content type.
Get onto "best of" lists and comparison articles in your niche.

Phase 4: Measure and iterate (monthly)
Re-run the prompt list every month and track share of voice versus competitors.
Correct wrong facts at the source. If AI gets something wrong, find where it came from (an old profile, outdated article) and fix it there.
Track AI referral traffic in analytics (referrers like chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai).
Refresh key pages with dates and new data every 2–3 months.

Rough effort split
Activity	Share of effort
Off-site mentions and authority	~50%
Content restructuring and original data	~30%
Technical (schema, crawlers, llms.txt)	~10%
Monitoring and correction	~10%

The key mindset shift: SEO is mostly work on your own site; 
GEO results come mostly from what the rest of the web says about you, 
because that's what models learn from and retrieve.
